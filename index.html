how it fits together

Three pieces, working only for you

Think of it as three small parts on your own computer. Each one has a simple job, and none of them phone home.

1

The extension in your browser

A lightweight browser add-on that captures and filters only what you choose to keep. You decide what it pays attention to — it ignores everything else.

2

The Vault app on your computer

A private, encrypted store that lives on your own machine. Your data is locked, and you hold the key — a master password only you know. Nothing is stored in the cloud.

3

The cortex the engine in the middle

The quiet engine that connects the two — and acts as a gatekeeper. It decides what's allowed to reach your Vault. Everything happens on your machine; nothing leaves it.

The flow is simple: you choose → the extension captures → the cortex checks it → your Vault keeps it, encrypted. Every step stays on your computer.

set it up

Get your vault running

A short, guided walkthrough — one small step at a time, with every download link you need. Made for everyone, not just techies.

1

Add the extension

The browser add-on that captures what you choose. Install it free from the Chrome Web Store: Add to Chrome ↗.

2

Download the Vault app

Your private, encrypted store for Mac — signed and Apple-notarized, so it opens cleanly with no warnings. Download it here: Download for Mac ↗, then drag it into your Applications folder.

3

Open it and set a password

Launch the Vault and choose a master password — your key, which never leaves your machine. You'll then get a Recovery Kit: three shares, any two of which reset your password if you ever forget it. Save them somewhere safe and separate — only you ever hold them; we never see or store them.

4

Connect the extension

In the Vault's dashboard, copy the access token. Open the extension's “Send to Vault” setting, paste it in, and save. Now the two are linked — privately, over your own computer only.

5

Capture live, and import your history

Turn on Capture and your AI chats — Claude, ChatGPT, and more — plus your browsing start flowing into the Vault as they happen. Already have history? In the Vault, click Import and load your Claude or ChatGPT export — your whole past corpus, in one go.

6

Get your free AI engine

Your AI runs on a small, free app on your Mac — nothing in the cloud, no account, and nothing to type. Download Ollama (free, here ↗) and open it. Pick a model from its list — Llama or Qwen are both great starting points — and it downloads itself the first time (one wait of a few minutes). That's the whole setup; the Vault finds it on its own, with nothing to configure.
Prefer a more visual, click-everything app? LM Studio ↗ works exactly the same way.

7

Talk to your own AI

In the Vault, open the My AI tab and ask anything. It answers from your corpus — and shows you the conversations it drew on — entirely on your machine. Everything you've thought through becomes a private AI that knows your life, plus the licensable personas you choose to share. Your data, your model, yours to keep.

One thing to remember: your Vault lives only on this computer. Nothing you capture ever leaves your machine — there's no account to sign up for and no server holding your data. Want the deeper details? The Vault docs and the GitHub org have everything.
